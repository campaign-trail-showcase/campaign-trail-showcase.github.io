You may think that I’m going to moralize on you that ‘Fallout during the Bethesda era’ is better or ‘Fallout during the Interplay/Black Isle/Obsidian era is better’. Or, you may think that I’m going to moralize about religion. Nope. There’s going to be implicit borrowing of arguments to create this universe, but, nowhere am I going to tell you: ‘This is why you should do this or that or think this way’. That’s for you to decide.

    In any case, this is going to be a dissection of the institutional-based perspective of the mod, A Steady Hand. And as such, ‘nerding out moments’ here. Is it ‘cringe’? Yeah.

    Do I give a shit?

    Let’s proceed.

    ‘What’s Up With The Timeline Divergence?’

    I can understand that there exist critics who may suggest that ‘Fallout diverged around 1947’ or ‘right after the end of the Second World War’. That’s a good point to make. Fallout’s aesthetic is stuck in the retro futuristic 1950s, i.e. everything looks and feels like you’re living in the 1950s. Eisenhower optimism and what not.

    I agree. The 1950s are a cool setting, not even discounting the retro futuristic 1950s setting in of itself. While the veneer is the 1950s, the problems within are definitively not from the 1950s.

    The phrase ‘A Steady Hand’ of the mod should suggest something closer to Eisenhower-level stability, where the era of Eisenhower was this blissful experience and everything ‘just made sense before everything went to shit during the 1960s’. The title is also fucking pretentious as hell.

    Everything looks great on the surface because the veneer reaches for that stable 1950s feeling. Underneath, the veneer is almost certainly not the 1950s.

    Someone once said online about how ‘Bethesda does not understand Fallout’. I’m not litigating on that front, nor am I going to say that ‘Bethesda is better’ or ‘old-school is better’. Instead, I’m going something that they used to describe ‘old-school’ Fallout. This person summed up ‘old-school’ Fallout’s world this way: “the world of Fallout was more in line with the 80s, the late Cold War, you know, with the computers and the weapons and all that. But culturally, they were in the midst of a period of 50s revivalism, sort of remembering better days at the time of the war.”

    I’m not here to change your opinion on when Pre-Great-War Fallout’s era diverged from our history. Just know that the basis of my mod assumes that everything up to the 1970s happened. Richard Nixon happened. George McGovern lost. Gerald Ford lost. Vietnam happened but America didn’t see it as a sort of ‘Vietnam syndrome’ but rather a ‘we could’ve won some more’ situation. This ‘thirst for victory’ syndrome would, as my game’s tooltip says, ‘A quagmire that set the stage for America's want for more war.’ America has progressed through history in a relatively similar fashion to the point that key structural beats of the mod (law and order rhetoric, convention troubles, corrupt deals) are lifted from these more turbulent eras.

    However, I am not going to necessarily claim that Ronald Reagan won in 1980. I am going to claim that the Enclave may have emerged during the period where America was de-regulating and building up the American military. In truth, my mod is not building a perfectly explored, everything-is-charted timeline of the divergent world of Fallout. You can headcanon it out, but, as for my mod, the world is somewhat 80s technology and lore but the backdrop is a forced bubble where people pretend that everything is stuck in the 1950s. Yeah, from 1980s all the way to the 2070s. 90 years of forced ‘everything is fine’.

    In this regard, A Steady Hand’s Pre-Great-War era is cheating with regards to keeping everything in the 50s. Yes, my mod is able to steal from real-world history and reference things that people generally don’t consider to be ‘true Fallout lore’. The premise still stands like this for my pre-Great-War Fallout mod, as was echoed in the intro of my mod: “The shining city upon a hill is a promised land. This promised land prefers to wrap itself in a safety blanket of a nostalgic, bygone age. The past is comfy. The problems of today don't really matter at all.”

    That’s the whole shtick of my mod’s encapsulation of Pre-Great-War Fallout. It is what forms the basis for the ‘eerily similar, but not exact’ match throughout the entirety of my mod. This philosophy is the underlying thread that lets institutions in my Pre-Great-War Fallout mod even have a common reservoir to draw from in the first place, which is 1950s to 1980s American history.

    ‘What’s Up With the Racism?’

    If you’ve played through the mod and chosen the ‘weaponize racism’ option (PK id 4523) or wondered about why there’s such a seemingly ‘gratuitous’ depiction of racism with slurs and racism throughout. Why is not-yet-radioactive Fallout suddenly getting into problems surrounding anti-Chinese racism?

    Good question. Also, good job if you’ve correctly sussed out that racism stinks.

    Here’s a definition created by me to bore you out:

    “Racism is harmful, regardless of what type it is because it is perpetuated based on wrongful schemas.”

    The thing about racism in ‘A Steady Hand’ is that there exists an explanation from an in-world view (and, by extension, a this is how the world sometimes works view) to explain WHY racism is showing up.

    I’m going to break it down into these four components. Starting with…

    1. ‘Weaponizing racism’.

    Weaponizing racism is triggered by selecting this answer on Question #7 (Answer PK 4523): “ Now, pardon me, but, really: Can't we lean into the fact that they are from the East and we are from the West? That was how we framed the fight against the Japanese in World War II. We can do it here too.”

    The corresponding feedback is this: “The ghosts of anti-Asian racist sentiments return to the land of the living. The pieces have already been set since these foreigners immigrated here: We just have to knock the Communist Asians down to a level of being less than human. Works just fine."

    Right away, the answer and feedback are making it clear that what the player is doing is not something new. In fact, I want to pull a page from u/BreadSanta1917’s mod, ‘1936 – March on Washington’ because March on Washington brilliantly managed to highlight anti-Asian discrimination during the 1930s.

    During the game of March on Washington, the player, as President Child, has to confront an issue related to immigration. The question itself posits the situation as such: “Amongst the various policies that your new party has chosen to espouse, immigration is by far one of the most pressing in the eyes of your base, with conservatives and nationalists up and down the country citing these new citizens as the cause of everything from economic woes to socialist agitation. The NEC has been itching to capture nativist support in the form of legislation for months now, only holding off due to the previously chaotic nature of your coalition. With the formation of the ALL, the battle lines in congress have been drawn, and Johnson is waiting for the green light to let loose an immigration bill into the house.”

    The answer that shows anti-Asian discrimination says the following: “I had only just returned to America when the Immigration Act of 1924 was passed, so I remember very clearly just how groundbreaking it was. We should use it as the basis for our act, with our focus being on ending any remaining east Asian immigration to this country. Laborers in the west will be thankful for our efforts to keep their jobs secure.”

    That game frames that choice as such with this feedback: “As the act ascends through the chambers, no one dares speak out in defense of the Asian - whether out of fear or racism, it does not matter. Ships carrying the victims of colonialism from India and Southeast Asia are turned back before they can dock, dooming thousands to a life of subjugation, all the while the Asian American communities on the West Coast become more and more isolated, scared to speak up lest they share the same fate.”

    Mechanically-wise, this option affects ‘IWWAgitation += 1;’ for March on Washington.

    In A Steady Hand, however, weaponizing racism has many downstream consequences.

    Firstly, if the player as Rodney wins against the Democratic opponent, the game supplies two slides that should override all the other achievements of a player: ‘The Orientals’, which is an in-universe propaganda piece that is derided by Military Police Armstrong and Higgins, and ‘Genetically Engineered Freak’, which depicts anti-Chinese racism transforming into anti-mutant racism.

    Let’s start off with a dissection of the first slide, ‘The Orientals’.

    This slide itself can be correctly construed as ‘hamfisted’. The point of the slide was to be an aggregation of ALL the wartime racism that Americans displayed to the Japanese during the Second World War, but now aimed at the Chinese.

    The three tropes employed are extremely simplistic because those are the quickest ways to turn someone who usually has no care for hating a specific race into someone who is willing to kill people associated with a specific race.

    For instance, the very first trope employed is that of ‘national innocence’ with ‘America did not ask to be attacked. America did not ask for war. America did not ask for hate.’ This relates to something that Racism in Japanese and U.S. Wartime Propaganda authored by Nancy Brcak and John R. Pavia describes as being a natural theme. Brcak and Pavia note that “The most natural theme in all modern propaganda is defense: no nation wants to be perceived as the aggressor.”

    The next trope is structurally a vague echo of all real-world racism: Othering. As the song from Pocahontas says, “They're not like you and me. Which means they must be evil.” This is patently false because this is merely a ‘vague feeling’ that the other person is different. Racism is a ‘schema’. It doesn’t need to be a ‘grounded’ feeling because racism is not grounded. There is no 100% accurate way to say that ‘because this group looks like this or acts like this, any person of this group MUST be like this.’ Exceptions are bound to occur and trying to intellectually flatten this down to a simple mental shortcut is how we get disagreements because people deliberately choose to not spend effort to learn about other people.

    The final trope is relatively simple as well. This is an 'appeal to authority' trope whereby the racists are trying to claim that a superior authority (i.e. 'pseudoscience of racism') have confirmed that the Chinese are going to act this way or that way. We should be aware that there is NO way to say that ALL members of a population are able to act this way or that way. The game mocks this claim of 'authority' by scare-quoting the authority while also declining to provide the 'expert opinion' judgement.

    The Military Police in the scene aren't meant to act as a voice piece for the author directly. Instead, these MPs don't care about the document nor do they spend time trying to litigate the document on its own terms. What they do care about, however, is the ripple effects of racism. The Military Police aren't pleased that, now, American soldiers want to 'get revenge' on the Chinese. Just because the Chinese prisoners were once the enemy does not mean that the Chinese should be subjected to torture and brutalization that violates the Geneva Convention.

    The second slide ‘Genetically Engineered Freak’ is meant to show that racism doesn’t need to have a fixed target. Mutants are easily targetable as Chinese people. The mod does not hate Chinese people, but rather, the mod wants to show the mechanisms that allow hatred to easily become propagated in the minds of people. Unfortunately, these mechanisms route through the human mind very simplistically by creating an ‘out-group’ to target, which routes through the human mind’s pre-built systems to identify that this or that is not in a person’s ‘in-group’ and, thus, should be regarded with suspicion.

    Do note also, ‘Genetically Engineered Freak’ is deliberately misrepresenting the American military. This is because the American military of the Enclave is a mishmash of the real United States Army and the United States Marine Corps. This fact also points to how the Enclave itself is a mishmash of radical racist fascists whose ideology is easily overridden by the ‘pragmatism’ of employing a mutant like Frank Horrigan.

    2. The second deal: Anti-Chinese racism perpetuated by American soldiers.

    Quick question: Do you know what the Americans called the Vietnamese during the Vietnam War? Afghanis and Iraqis during the Afghanistan and Iraq War?

    I’m not going to give you the answer, but, what I am going to tell you is that despite these being comparatively ‘low-level’, as in not total war mobilization eras, American soldiers still dehumanized their opponents.

    Think of it this way: Humans are not quite fond of killing a human being. However, dehumanizing the enemy with slurs and other such tactics helps close (but never necessarily fully close) the canyon between reluctance to kill and to kill. At the same time, dehumanization unfortunately helps to cope with the fact that soldiers are being told to go out into war and accept death as a potential consequence of their profession. Given enough constant exposure to danger, soldiers are more likely to start showing dehumanization tendencies to people who they once fought. However, this should not be interpreted as a sweeping generalization that ALL soldiers hate the enemy. What I am instead claiming is that soldiers MAY often dehumanize. This dehumanization often appears in derisive language towards the enemy, which is something that my mod depicts with regards to American soldiers showing disdain for their Chinese counterparts, regardless of whether racism has been weaponized or not.

    The third deal: Anti-Chinese racism as being toxic to diplomacy.

    How can you potentially strike a deal with someone who thinks you aren’t even human? How can you potentially negotiate with those who don’t even think you’re worthy of being negotiated with?

    The easy answer is that it is meaningfully far too difficult because negotiation requires some semblance of trust. If a nation state thinks that your nation state don’t deserve to live and don’t deserve the mercy of negotiation, then trying to waste effort in appeasing them,

    This is why weaponizing racism during the course of the campaign often leads to a player sinking any chances of negotiations, whether it be with the ‘DealFakeouts’ or with genuine peace deals. The Chinese have no incentive to negotiate with a power that seems to want to destroy them.

    The fourth deal: Weaponizing racism has the ability to completely backfire on you by exposing a monster that is worse than what ordinary humans are.

    I will admit that the ‘Nixon’ subplot is fundamentally very shitposty. However, the basic mechanism is that, because you demonized Chinese people, under the rare circumstances (i.e. RNGDeterminer being less than -0.2), you will encounter a question where you as Rodney might doubt that the American political system is as it seems.

    If you doubt the American political system, you will trigger a branch that shows Richard Milhous Nixon running as a Democrat with a genderswapped Robert F. Kennedy.

    The scenario itself is deliberately absurd and can also be read as a joke about ‘Things that Never Were’. Despite this however, the main point is that weaponizing racism has the potential for the player to see that demonizing humans may, as a sort of narrative payback per se, result in even worse monsters arriving to make your life more miserable.

    ‘Why the hell are the U.S. Army and Marine Corps depicted so strangely?’

    "Ooorah. I love the smell of napalm in the morning. I am generic American Marine Corps soldier."

    "Woe is me. The war is so bad. War is hell, man. Total hell. I hate war..."

    These two stances are both stances that I did not want to embody in my work. As part of my institutional critique during this mod, I had to do some bit of research (through running my tex through pattern-matching LLMs lol to verify my text but also watching Youtube videos of the USMC) about how to convey a 'somewhat realisticish' United States military.

    First things first. The United States military in this mod? It is not entirely Vietnam-War-era military's institution. Nor is it entirely the Korean-War-era military's institution. Nor is it entirely the Second World War's military institution. Sure, there's some slang from those eras... 'Fucking New Guy' (FNG), repple depples (WW2 era slang), flyboys (WW2 era slang). However, the REAL military in this Pre-Great-War timeline is that of the 'Thank You For Your Service' military. You know: The soldiers and Marines who went to fight in the GWOT and still serving even to this day.

    The reason for the TYFYS era of military is two-fold. First of all, my sources are garbage. No, I didn't read a memoir. Nor did I read veterans' accounts of stuff. As such, to those who can claim higher authority on 'knowing what the real US Army/USMC' is like? Congratulations. You've seen the military first-hand and how incompetent it can be sometimes as an institution.

    The second reason for the TYFYS era of military is that TYFYS era is simply the register that civilians, such as myself, are accustomed to. All the talk of warfighting and what not isn't the old-school talk of 'military? That's a job when you can't find a job'. No, now, the military hs to be 'thank for your service' and we have to give tokens of appreciation for soldiers. The truth is that warfighting is still a job at the end of the day. I reject the heroic mythology of the American military because the 'green weenie' (the sheer feeling of the military institution screwing you over) is what gets commended when we talk about the military as a heroic institution. The men and women who have volunteered to fight are the ones, as actual human beings, deserve to be more than just 'generic soldier.

    Now, onto the really nerdy stuff...

    'Why are the Army and Marines sometimes portrayed as a 'volunteer-force' and sometimes as a 'conscripted force?'

    Answer: Because this is a fuck-up on my end. Yeah, it's a fuck-up, but, I don't give a shit. However, if you do try to extract some kernels of truth from this mod whenever you read about Vernon the Marine President, Sinclair the Army President, Nicholas Neville the Marine, or any of the grunts, just know that most of the time (except for Private Dunbar, lucky conscripted bastaard), the institutional detail of the Army/Marine Corps BEST describes the present-day 'TYFYS, all-volunteer force'. All of it: The four year contracts, the fire team compositions (except for Corporal Pete Franklin because his fire team is out of regs, but, he just doesn't give a shit), military unit layering (except for Barry Locke's unit. His is a division that was never properly constituted in relaity), Non-Commissioned Officer dynamic differences in the U.S. Army vs that of the U.S. Marine Corps, and overall 'aggregated' (albeit never going to be a 100% accurate and comprehensive portrayal of the U.S. military by any meaningful meaning) view of the U.S. military. All of it is something that can somewhat GENERALLY help explain the United States military as an institution. As a side note, you, if you are fluent in the TO&E (Table of Organization and Equipment) of the modern-day U.S. Army might object to me forcing the Army to abide by a 'division struture' rather than the brigade structure of Afghanistan and Iraq. To that point, yes, you're correct on that narrow sliver of 'technically brigade'. For my side, however, I was working on 'pattern-matching' about how Army units were constituted during the Korean War, which was in the form of divisions. Also, your point wouldn't work for the U.S. when it's fighting a peer adversary. Brigades are able to fight better in an environment such as insurgency-level situations. Divisions are better for peer-conflicts, which is why, according to AFCEA.org... 'The U.S. Army Turns Focus to the Division Level'. So, yeah, the Army is turning back to the divison model and isn't retaining the brigade model."

    Overall, the military to me is nothing special. To claim that it is a 'warfighting' institution that is the 'rah' and 'greatest fighting force' isn't a claim that matters much to me during the making of this mod. Instead, I was more interested in producing competent soldiers who are governed by a totalizing institution.

    'Are Sinclair and Vernon broken veterans?'

    No. I don't want to claim that Vernon and Sinclair are broken veterans who have an impulse for anger and are generally volatile people. Vernon and Sinclair are veterans who have been shaped by their service. Do not read them as a 'perfect depiction of every veteran ever'. Rather, they are veterans who have absorbed the institution of the U.S. Army for Sinclair and U.S. Marine Corps for Vernon. These men are meant to be men who, though changed by their service, should be defined mainly by being competent despite their baggage. In this regard, I wanted to portray veterans who are able to still get shit done rather than become 'oh woe is me' or 'i am aggressively angry'. Veterans are still people who oftentimes ARE able to live a life. The qualifcation is that 'oftentimes' as personal circumstances can vary from veteran to veteran.

    ‘What's up with the diplomacy?’

    Ok, here's a 'not-so-secret' that I'm going to let you in on. Ready?

    This is not real-life diplomacy. This is not how any of real-life diplomacy works. This is actually more akin to an interpersonal sort of diplomacy where people can negotiate with each other. There are two primary reasons for modeling nation-state diplomacy as if it was fundamentally equivalent to interpersonal diplomacy.

    Constructing 3 institutions from scratch is difficult.

    Diplomacy is a strange sort of thing. On the surface, it looks like some people talking about issues. Not necessarily thrilling stuff for a drama that wants to be realistic.

    Negotiating with a person is 'relatively' simple compared to negotiating with an institution like a nation-state. A nation-state experiences constraints that people may not typically experience. A nation-state's stated position to another nation-state can often be considered the 'sum' of colective interests of numerous institutions that make up the nation-state, such as the citzenry, the military, the corporations, and many other stakeholders. At the same time, nation-states may have the problem of 'face' where they can't necessarily show themselves to be weak, even when we, as outsiders, may think that showing weakness MIGHT be better off for the nation in terms of reaching agreeemnts. But no, a nation-state sometimes can't afford to do so because of potential fears that a nation might be taken advantage of or even because the converging interests of the nation-state's institutions simply don't allow for weakness to be shown.

    The issue with modelliing diplomacy is that you have to create THREE institutions: One for the United States, one for China, and one for the intersection between the United Staes and China WITHIN THE NEGOTIATION ITSELF. To do so, you will have to consider what the converging interests are for a nation-state such as the United States. AND THEN, you also have to consider the converging nation-state interests of the People's Republic of China. AND FINALLY, you have to consider how these two seemingly 'monolthic' interests and positions collide when we force both to interact with each other in the ring of diplomacy. This is something that is FAR TOO HIGH above my paygrade to actually complete. This is not also discounting the fact that if we WERE to consider 'converging interests', well, the Enclave of the United States and the CCP of the PRC probably don't have a good reason to allow a deal to go through in the first place. As such, I would think that diplomacy, if I tried to put in the effort to accurately model diplomacy (which is still way above my paygrade), would perhaps produce something that goes 'no deal whatsoever'. Obviously, this isn't fun for me and might not be fun for you, as a player, either.

    Falling back onto material that I am already familiar with.

    Because the 'creating 3 institutions from scratch' problem is far too difficult for me, like I have done through this mod, I have chosen to cheat with modelling. I have chosen to model the United States as if the Ambassador-at-Large, Bernard Reinhardt, and Ambassador-at-Large, Gao Qiang, (yes, I use Ambassadors-at-Large as an echo of the peace deal negotiations of LBJ during 1968) are able to fully embody and represent the positions and interests of their respective countries. This is flattening with regards to realism, however, this flattening allows me to use material that I already have some familiarity with with regards to interpersonal negotiation to then construct the actual diplomatic mechanics for the mod ON TOP of the diplomacy ending slides. I don't really like the hand-waving of diplomacy in a sense of 'and then they somehow got a deal' but I seemingly don't have the skill to actually properly execute real depictions of diplomacy. As such, this awkward middle ground of 'legibile negotiation theory, but, terrible real diplomacy' is what I landed on. It sucks, but, well, I really wanted to depict diplomacy.

    'What's With the Notes Function on the Rodney and Neville sides?'

    I have read on the Campaign Trail Discord that some people may think that the notes feature is useless. Well, yeah, it is useless if you don't open it. The point of the notes feature is track events that are considered 'major' or 'important' according to me, the author. The notes feature is supposed to track what happens over the course of a campaign on either the Rodney or Neville side. Mechanically, the notes are rather simple: See if a variable matches a specific number and then output some flavor text for the player to read. I found the idea of tracking what happens during a player's game (and being able to see as if the game is 'reacting' (although it isn't really reacting dynamically with a system in place but rather, by hand-authored content) interesting (and adopted this notes section feature from 2016: Tough As Texas' variable tracker). The notes section is used quite a bit to write some goofy stuff during the Nixon subplot though of the game, though.

Now, really, only the console and such stuff is disabled and can only be 'returned back' to the game when you've achieved both at least 1 'true' and 1 'shaky' peace ending deal to unlock these locked features on Rodney's side. For Neville's side, to receive the locked features, try to balance out your coalition such that you don't suffer any coalition ruptures during your campaign.
